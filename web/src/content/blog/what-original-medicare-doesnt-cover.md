---
title: "What Original Medicare doesn't cover"
description: "The gaps people find out about at the worst possible moment, and what each one is usually filled with."
date: 2026-06-23
tag: "Coverage basics"
---

Original Medicare, Parts A and B together, covers a great deal. What surprises
people is the list of everyday things it doesn't. Here's that list, and what
usually fills each gap.

## Prescriptions you pick up at a pharmacy

Part B covers some drugs administered in a clinical setting, but the medicines
you collect yourself generally aren't covered. That's what Part D is for, either
as a standalone plan or built into a Medicare Advantage plan.

Worth knowing: going without prescription coverage when you're first eligible can
add a permanent penalty to your premium later. It isn't a one-time fee; you keep
paying it. If you take no medications today, that's exactly the situation the
penalty catches out.

## Routine dental, vision and hearing care

Cleanings, glasses, hearing aids and the exams that go with them aren't covered
by Original Medicare. Some Medicare Advantage plans include benefits for them,
and standalone dental and vision plans can be bought separately at more or less
any time of year.

## Long-term custodial care

Help with daily living (bathing, dressing, eating) isn't covered when that's
the only care needed. Medicare does cover skilled nursing care after a qualifying
hospital stay, which is a narrower thing than most people assume it to be.

## Care outside the United States

Coverage abroad is very limited. Some Medicare Supplement plans include a foreign
travel emergency benefit, up to the plan's limits, which is worth knowing about
if you travel.

## Your share of the bill, without a ceiling

This is the gap people underestimate. Original Medicare pays most of a covered
bill and leaves you deductibles, copayments and coinsurance, and on its own,
there is no annual limit on how much that adds up to.

The two ways of dealing with that are the whole Supplement-versus-Advantage
decision. A Supplement pays that share for you. An Advantage plan puts a yearly
cap on it. They work differently and you choose one.

## The short version

Nothing on this list is a flaw in Medicare. They're just the edges of what it
does, and each has a well-worn way of being handled. Knowing where they are
before you need to is the entire point of having this conversation early.
